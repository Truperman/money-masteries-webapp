I'm Doctor Gainz. Lesson 23: Evaluate costs before offering a
service. A responsible business plan includes training time, secure tools,
administrative work, professional review, permitted marketing, and the
possibility that no paid work results.
A potential case amount is not your revenue.
Your compensation depends on lawful, reviewed arrangements and actual
completed work under those arrangements.
For a fictional budgeting exercise, assume two hundred dollars of tools and
three hundred dollars of other fixed monthly costs.
At a hypothetical two hundred fifty dollars contribution after case-specific
costs, two completed paid services would cover those fixed costs
arithmetically. This is a break-even illustration only.
It supplies no evidence of customer demand, permitted fee levels,
probability of payment, or likely monthly earnings.
Research local limitations before committing to spending.
Keep an explicit stop condition for a model that is restricted, uneconomic,
or dependent on unsupported income claims.
Learning a skill does not create a guaranteed market for it.

Quick knowledge check. What does the break-even example establish?
The answer: An arithmetic relationship under stated fictional assumptions.
Business arithmetic is not evidence of lawful pricing, demand, or future
results. Keep the source, the date, and the unknowns visible.
