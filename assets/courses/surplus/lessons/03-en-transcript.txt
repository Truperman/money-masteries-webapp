I'm Doctor Gainz. Lesson 3: Define a service that creates real value.
Useful support can include organizing records a client supplies, tracking
missing information, explaining the source of a status update, and
coordinating a professional review.
That value depends on accurate work and permitted activities.
Locating a possible fund does not automatically authorize you to represent a
claimant, charge a fee, purchase rights, or file papers.
Write a one-page service description: what you do, what you do not decide,
what you charge only if permitted and agreed, and when you refer the matter.
Explain that an owner may have a direct claim route.
Show any prospective client where the official process is described, without
suggesting that your business is the government or that payment to you is
mandatory. Business income is uncertain.
Training, tools, legal review, and time are costs.
Avoid advertising a typical recovery or monthly income unless you have a
sound basis for the specific claim.
This course teaches a process, not an income promise.

Draft a plain-language service description with a direct-claim disclosure

Quick knowledge check. Which service description is appropriate?
The answer: We organize authorized records and coordinate permitted
assistance; outcomes require verification.
Describe the actual work and its limits rather than claiming control over a
government disbursement. Keep the source, the date, and the unknowns
visible. I'll see you in the next lesson.
