Use illustrative surplus arithmetic.
For a classroom example, assume sale proceeds of $225 ,000,
documented sale costs of $18 ,000, and a documented secured obligation of
$172 ,000. The arithmetic residual is $35 ,000.
This is an illustration of subtraction, not a determination that an owner
is presently owed that amount.
Real cases require the applicable accounting, priorities, and current
balance. If a qualified review later establishes a further $13 ,000
distribution in the same fictional example, the illustrative residual after
that allocation is $22 ,000.
Do not subtract a disputed claim as though it is an approved payment, or
ignore it as though it has no possible effect.
