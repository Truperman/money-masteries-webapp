I'm Doctor Gainz. Lesson 20: Build a source-constrained case brief.
A useful case brief separates confirmed facts, estimates, questions, and
next actions. Every financial figure should carry a label, source, effective
date, and reviewer status.
Keep the original document reference beside the summary so another person
can check it without reconstructing your reasoning.
Use this original prompt with fictional or authorized material: “Summarize
only the supplied records.
Create sections for process, authority, accounting, events, unresolved
issues, and requested review.
Attach source references. Mark conflicts.
Do not search for private people, fabricate missing data, rank legal rights,
or contact anyone.” Check the result line by line before sharing it.
Be especially careful with current-tense statements.
“The ledger recorded a deposit on a date” is different from “The money
remains available.” A brief should preserve that distinction and make stale
sources easy to spot.
Pause and try this.
Write a one-page fictional brief with two source-linked facts, one estimate,
three unresolved questions, and a specific handoff request.
Quick knowledge check. Which brief is easiest to audit?
The answer: A structured summary separating sourced facts and unresolved
questions. Source references and clear uncertainty let a reviewer verify the
work. Keep the source, the date, and the unknowns visible.
