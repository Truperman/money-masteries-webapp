I'm Doctor Gainz. Lesson 12: Track claims, distributions, and deadlines.
A filed motion or claim is not necessarily an approved distribution.
Similarly, an approved distribution may have been paid, stayed, corrected,
or challenged. Keep an event log that distinguishes filed, pending, ordered,
disbursed, and unresolved statuses.
Use the language of the source and preserve any qualification.
For each important date, record the triggering event, the source, and who
confirmed its significance. Flag a deadline that requires professional
interpretation. Keep status history instead of overwriting a former amount
without explanation. Where a source is old, closed, or access-limited, make
that limitation visible at the top of the file.
No general time promise is appropriate for every claim.
A useful update states what was checked, when it was checked, what remains
unknown, and the permitted next action.
Avoid telling a client that money is on its way because a document was
submitted. Pause and try this.
Write a fictional event log containing a claim, a competing claim, and an
unresolved hearing. Draft an accurate two- sentence status update.
Quick knowledge check. A claim was filed yesterday.
What status is established?
The answer: Filed; outcome remains unverified.
Filing records establish that an event occurred, not that entitlement or
payment was decided. Keep the source, the date, and the unknowns visible.
