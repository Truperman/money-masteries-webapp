I'm Doctor Gainz. Lesson 5: Check record- use and fee restrictions first.
Access to government information does not automatically permit every use of
it. King County’s surplus page expressly warns against commercial use of
lists of individuals. South Carolina law restricts knowingly obtaining or
using personal government-record information for commercial solicitation.
These are examples, not a complete national rule map.
Keep a jurisdiction requirements sheet with the exact official source, the
date reviewed, the activity being considered, and the unresolved question.
Check access attestations, solicitation, representation, licensing,
compensation, assignments, and retention.
Do not click an acknowledgement that conflicts with your purpose or use an
agent to cross a gate you could not truthfully accept yourself.
Fee rules may make a proposed business model unsuitable.
Jefferson County’s page includes a waiting-period warning for finder fees.
Read current authority with a qualified local professional before adopting
any fee structure. A course example from another state is not permission.
Pause and try this.
Record two published restrictions from official sources.
Describe how each changes your proposed activity, without collecting any
claimant information. Quick knowledge check.
An access gate prohibits commercial use of individual lists.
What should an agent do?
The answer: Stop that use and record the restriction.
Automation must respect the same access and use restrictions as the person
directing it. Keep the source, the date, and the unknowns visible.
