I'm Doctor Gainz. Lesson 6: Build a jurisdiction checklist.
A useful checklist records who holds the funds, how the relevant dates are
measured, who may submit a claim, what evidence is required, and where
disputes are decided. Add whether a business entity needs counsel and
whether a proposed assignment or fee arrangement has special requirements.
A checklist with unknown answers is more useful than a confident guess.
For each requirement, retain a short note and an official source link.
Label the requirement as confirmed for the specific sale type, awaiting
clarification, or outside your role.
Do not copy an old deadline into a new case without verifying what event
starts the clock. Source publication dates, sale dates, and claim deadlines
are different fields. Keep the checklist current without rewriting the
underlying evidence. When guidance changes, preserve the former version date
and explain what needs professional review.
Your checklist organizes research; it does not decide a legal question.
Pause and try this.
Build a blank checklist with ten requirements.
Fill only facts that a current official source actually establishes.
Leave the rest awaiting review.
Quick knowledge check. Which date should automatically be used as the claim
deadline? The answer: Neither; verify the applicable event and rule.
Different claim procedures use different triggering events and periods.
Keep the source, the date, and the unknowns visible.
